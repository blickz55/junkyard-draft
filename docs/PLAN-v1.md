# Make Every Verified Run Worth Sharing

*Junkyard Draft design research and versioned plan, v0.3 → v1.0. Research notes gathered 2026-10-25; repository state read from `docs/DESIGN.md`, `docs/ROADMAP.md`, `docs/STATUS.md` (0.2.0 baseline), `CHANGELOG.md` and `docs/CONTINUE.md`.*

Junkyard Draft already owns the hardest asset in this genre: a pure, integer-safe simulation whose every run is a seed plus a rule-checked action list that a server re-simulates before any score is called verified. The research says the winning move is not more machines or live multiplayer but to build outward from that asset in a specific order: make the first five minutes legible (v0.3), turn the 80-tick run into the spectacle and the social artefact (v0.4: histogram, loop export, spoiler-free share grid), add one new decision at a time behind `RULES_VERSION` bumps (v0.5), ship a daily that nobody is punished for missing (v0.6), add asynchronous social play that works at ten players (v0.7), harden determinism and platforms (v0.8), run a beta with integrity operations (v0.9) and launch (v1.0). The measured evidence is thin but points one way: forgiving streaks outperform strict ones (**Duolingo's streak decoupling lifted D14 retention 3.3%** ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak))), a spoiler-free, link-free share block took Wordle from **90 to roughly 300,000 daily players in two months** ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2022/01/31/business/new-york-times-buys-wordle-push-expand-its-games-business)), per-level histograms plus GIF export are the social layer that kept Zachtronics players returning to solved puzzles ([Wikipedia: Infinifactory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinifactory); [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/558990/opus_magnum.md)), and every multiplayer format that consumes *recorded* play works at any population while every live format needs concurrency a solo indie will not have ([Wikipedia: Super Auto Pets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Auto_Pets); [Chucklefish](https://chucklefish.org/?p=1295)). The single discipline that holds the plan together is seed fairness: nothing account-level may change what a daily seed offers, scores only ever improve with more attempts, and skipping a day costs nothing but that day's rank. Every feature below is sized so the nightly cloud routine can take it as a STATUS "Next task" (S = one routine run, M = two to four runs, L = five or more runs or needs a human, marked ⚑).

## Executive summary: five bets, in the order they pay off

**Bet 1 — The run is the show.** Designers of Balatro and Slay the Spire both reach for the same phrase, the "Rube Goldberg machine" you build and then watch go ([Game Developer: Slay the Spire](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-mega-crit-games-i-slay-the-spire-i-); [GMTK Substack](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/balatros-cursed-design-problem)), and Dinosaur Polo Club calls the pleasure of many predictable agents moving through a network you built "the bread and butter of our style of games" ([Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/hustle-and-bustle-how-mini-motorways-built-its-wuselfaktor/)). An 80-tick run on a 48-cell grid where every item is on screen is structurally perfect for this, and the existing replay viewer means a looping export of the final round is a small step rather than a new system. GIF export is one of the most-praised Opus Magnum features in a review-theme analysis (**weight 0.21**) ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/558990/opus_magnum.md)). v0.4 makes the money counter the heartbeat, exports the loop, and shows the score against a distribution.

**Bet 2 — Histogram first, verified only, unlimited practice, forgiving streak.** With 10 to 500 daily players a global rank is informative only at the top; a histogram with "you are here" reads the same at N=30 and N=30,000, which is exactly how Opus Magnum and Mini Motorways present scores ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/558990/discussions/0/1742229167201633990); [Dinosaur Polo Club](https://dinopoloclub.com/?p=1871)). The attempt policy should be Dead Cells' rather than Spelunky's: unlimited attempts, best verified score counts, because verification removes the cheating concern that made unlimited attempts "easily abusable" elsewhere ([Game Developer: The 24-hour ticket](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-24-hour-ticket-examining-daily-runs-)). Streaks count days played with a 48-hour grace (Chess.com's model) and never zero visible history ([Chess.com support](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8708990-how-does-the-daily-puzzle-work)).

**Bet 3 — Depth by one decision at a time, never by unlocks that touch the daily.** Zachtronics designs a "minimum set of tools that create emergent properties when used together" ([Game Developer: Opus Magnum](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-zachtronics-i-opus-magnum-i-)); the press balance pass in `docs/STATUS.md` shows the project already has the tooling to admit one machine at a time on evidence. Analysts attribute Balatro's weaker long-tail retention to the game feeling solved once a strategy is known ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250207-balatro-successful-launch-strategy)); the cheap, seed-fair antidote is Super Auto Pets' pattern of a fixed weekly rotation everyone shares ([Wikipedia: Super Auto Pets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Auto_Pets)), not account unlocks. Balatro disables unlocks in seeded runs for exactly this reason ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/best-balatro-seeds/)).

**Bet 4 — First five minutes: one rule per step, one guidance channel.** Factorio's own post-mortem found its tutorial took **30–45 minutes to reach automation** and taught players "how to solve the tutorial rather than ... the concepts" ([Factorio FFF #241](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-241)); its later fix gave each information channel one job and made the objective window sufficient on its own ([Factorio FFF #342](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-342)). Patrick's Parabox teaches at most one concept per level with early layouts the player "can't easily mess up" ([Tutorial Design of Patrick's Parabox](https://siddarthrg.substack.com/p/tutorial-design-of-patricks-parabox)). Junkyard Draft's orientation rule is its belt-lane problem; v0.3 gives it port arrows, a one-line rejection reason, undo, and a three-round tutorial seed.

**Bet 5 — Integrity by design, because the autoplayer ships with the game.** Re-simulation eliminates forged scores but, as Trackmania's decade-long scandal showed, it cannot tell a human from a tool ([Wikipedia: Wirtual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirtual)). The accepted community answer is separate human and tool-assisted boards with visible provenance ([TASVideos](https://tasvideos.org/Forum/Topics/22766)), a server-issued seed so nothing can be precomputed before the window opens (Wordle moved answers server-side after its client list leaked ([sbplat/wordle](https://github.com/sbplat/wordle))), and golden-replay tests across engines because `Math.sin`/`cos` differ even between V8 versions ([perplexdotgg/bounce issue #8](https://codeberg.org/perplexdotgg/bounce/issues/8)). The sim already avoids every transcendental function (checked: `game/src/sim/` contains no `Math.sin/cos/random`, `Date` or `sort`), so this bet is mostly tests and policy.

## Design principles the research supports

**Input randomness only; the simulation is the spreadsheet.** Keith Burgun's distinction is the clearest statement: output randomness is "noise injected between the player's decision and the outcome" and single-player games "require input randomness" ([Game Developer: Randomness and Game Design](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/randomness-and-game-design)); Into the Breach went to "great lengths to ensure the player could understand what was happening at all times" ([Into the Spine](https://intothespine.com/2019/05/06/into-the-breach-and-imperfection/)). Junkyard Draft's draft offers are the only randomness and the sim is fully legible, which already resolves LocalThunk's "cursed problem" of wanting slot-machine excitement with spreadsheet predictability ([GMTK Substack](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/balatros-cursed-design-problem)): the player cannot preview the exact 80-tick result, but every rule is knowable. Keep it that way — no "dry run" button, no hidden rolls inside a round.

**Footprint and facing are the scoring constraints; do not add an area score.** Infinifactory scores footprint, cycles and blocks ([Wikipedia: Infinifactory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinifactory)) and Kaizen makes tools occupy planning-grid cells to force the trade-off ([Slant Magazine](https://www.slantmagazine.com/games/kaizen-a-factory-story-review)); on an 8×6 grid area is already the binding constraint and the "behind only" input rule is the small-grid analogue of Factorio's lane geometry. The genre's most generative archetypes are splitter variants (jam-on-full versus overflow, a debate Factorio players had explicitly ([Factorio forums](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?p=352785))), mergers and filters — and a filter "only earns its slot if there are ≥2 item kinds in flight", which Junkyard Draft's five materials satisfy.

**Juice echoes the mechanics and scales with magnitude; the most frequent interaction must feel best.** Dorfromantik's rule that "the interaction the player does the most has to feel as satisfying as possible" ([Game Developer: Dorfromantik](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/sparking-joy-through-tile-placement-in-idyllic-village-builder-i-dorfromantik-i-)), Mini Metro's fully procedural event-driven soundscape ([Game Developer: Mini Metro postmortem](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/postmortem-dinosaur-polo-club-s-i-mini-metro-i-)) and Lovato's warning that excess juice "will both waste your time and annoy your users" ([Game Developer: Squeezing more juice](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/squeezing-more-juice-out-of-your-game-design-)) agree. The peer-reviewed support is thinner than the folklore — the CHI PLAY 2019 study on juicy design starts from the admission that "empirical evidence to back the assumption is lacking" ([CORE](https://core.ac.uk/works/8641643)) — so treat juice as a hypothesis to measure against first-session completion, not a certainty.

**Reach the loop in minutes, teach one rule per step through one channel, gate tips by what the player just did.** Beyond Factorio and Parabox, Chris Zukowski's survey of Next Fest developers found top demos often had no tutorial beyond a controls popup and that "you have to assume you have players' attention for minutes" ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/06/24/do-demos-help-earn-wishlists-steam-next-fest-june-2024/)). Factorio's tips system shows the model for a canvas game: tips unlock by dependency, suggestions fire from what the player does, static screenshots were replaced by live in-engine simulations because screenshots went stale ([Factorio FFF #361](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-361)).

**One ranked axis, shown as a distribution, friends and near-peers first.** The largest cluster of Opus Magnum complaints (**0.37**) is that three un-maxable graphs cause "repetitive replays and self-doubt" ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/558990/opus_magnum.md)); the early Opus Magnum cycle board broke ties by cost then area rather than running parallel ladders ([Biggie Blog](https://biggieblog.com/?p=836)). Slay the Spire 2 defaulted to friends' daily scores after global boards filled with implausible totals ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2868840/discussions/0/798966745989725135/)). A Topcoder quasi-experiment found relative scoring raised effort for top performers but lowered it for bottom performers ([LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/115983)), which argues for percentile and "around me" views over the gap to the leader.

**Same board for everyone.** Spelunky's daily exists to remove "most of the elements of randomness that make it difficult to accurately compare the skill of two different players" ([Spelunky wiki](https://spelunky.fandom.com/wiki/Daily_Challenge_Mode_%28HD%29)); Slay the Spire's daily unlocks everything "because it would imbalance the score" ([Steam Community thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/3667553591702749704)). Any unlock, pity timer, pick-steering or rubber-banded target that makes two players on one seed see different offers or targets breaks the promise even when it is technically deterministic.

**Nothing is lost by skipping a day.** The temporal dark-pattern line, as drawn by the darkpattern.games taxonomy used in a 2025 study of 1,496 mobile games, is "playing by appointment" and daily rewards that "penalise absence" ([arXiv 2412.05039](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05039v1)); seven studies found a broken streak drives less engagement than an intact one, with the gap shrinking when the streak can be repaired ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://udspace.udel.edu/handle/19716/34160)). Duolingo's own data say giving learners the option to take a break makes them "more likely to do more in the long run" ([Duolingo blog, streaks](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals)).

**Asynchronous beats live at this population.** Super Auto Pets performs all battles asynchronously against stored teams and falls back to AI teams "when no players are available" ([Wikipedia: Super Auto Pets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Auto_Pets)); Stardew Valley's live multiplayer required an outside programmer who "rewrote over 15,000 lines of code" ([Chucklefish](https://chucklefish.org/?p=1295)); shapez 2 reached 1.0 after 700K+ Early Access sales with no official multiplayer ([COGconnected](https://cogconnected.com/2026/04/shapez-2-1-0-launches-on-pc-following-an-impressive-700k-early-access-sales/)). Every social feature in this plan consumes a stored (seed, actions) pair; none needs two humans online.

**Verification is structural but not sufficient.** Opus Magnum's community shows the "Top Percentile" value 1% down the sorted list because the raw #1 "may very well be a cheated score" ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/playing-opus-magnum-by-different-rules/)); speedrun.com requires impartial moderators and lets any run be disputed ([speedrun.com Moderation Rules](https://speedrun.com/support/learn/moderation-rules)); Trackmania's report flow gives no outcome feedback and the community found it unsatisfying ([doc.trackmania.com](https://doc.trackmania.com/general/how-to-report-player/)). Keep raw replays, publish reason codes, split human from assisted play, and show percentile rather than the raw top score.

## The versioned plan: eight milestones from v0.3 to v1.0

How to read the milestone sections: each feature is numbered so dependencies can point at it, carries a size (**S** fits one nightly routine run and one pull request; **M** two to four runs; **L** five or more runs, or needs a human or another machine, marked ⚑), and ends with acceptance criteria a test or a measurement can check. Features are in priority order within a milestone. Any feature that changes a rule or a tuning value bumps `RULES_VERSION` and invalidates older replays and saves on purpose, as `CHANGELOG.md` already states; milestones that bump rules say so in their theme line. Two gates sit outside the code: a human must play and write `docs/PLAYTEST.md` before v0.3 is called done, and a human must host the verifier before any v0.4 population feature can be observed with real players. What already exists in 0.2.0 (keyboard placement, reduced motion, text size, WCAG audit, daily board, replay viewer, share text, display name, installer, verifier bundle and Dockerfile) is not re-planned; every feature below builds on it.

### v0.3 — First five minutes (no rules change)

**Theme.** A new player reaches a finished first run in under three minutes, understands every placement rejection, and can undo a mistake. The current STATUS "Next task" (manual playtest, then onboarding copy and a second tutorial seed) is the entry point; the features below are what the research says to build regardless of what the playtest adds.

**0.3.1 Manual playtest round one** — L ⚑. *What:* four or five fresh, non-expert testers, 20–30 minutes each, playing the current first run and one daily while thinking aloud; screen and audio recorded; developer silent unless the tester is stuck for more than two minutes; every recording watched in full before anything changes; findings tallied by category into `docs/PLAYTEST.md`; only patterns seen in two or more testers become tasks. *Why:* 3–5 testers per iteration is the documented sweet spot because beyond five "problems hide behind other problems" ([Game Developer: How many players should you playtest with](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-many-players-should-you-playtest-with-)); Patrick Traynor ran ~15 narrated video playtests with fresh testers each time and "did not reach diminishing returns" ([GDC 2024 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2024/Slides/GDC+slide+presentations/Traynor_Patrick_SystemCentricPuzzle.pdf)); players soften criticism and may call a game fun while silently struggling, so observation beats forms ([Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/comparing-player-testing-avoid-false-positives)). *Accept:* `docs/PLAYTEST.md` exists with per-tester time-to-first-run, count of rejected placements before the first valid one, and a ranked confusion list; at least two confusions map to tasks 0.3.2–0.3.6 or new STATUS items.

**0.3.2 Placement ghost with port arrows and a one-line rejection reason** — S. *What:* while a machine is in hand (mouse or keyboard cursor), draw its input side(s) and output side on the ghost; when a drop is refused, show the reason in the hint strip for ~2 s ("Crusher takes items from behind only — the hopper is beside it"; "cell occupied"; "outside the grid"), announced through the same text the screen-reader mirror will later read. *Why:* Factorio chose tip topics from what "players actually find confusing" and belt lanes and splitters top that list ([Factorio FFF #361](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-361)); Dorfromantik guides repeatedly flag edge-matching rules as the main hurdle ([Pro Game Guides](https://progameguides.com/dorfromantik/dorfromantik-tips-tricks-for-beginners/)); shapez's clog-stops-counter design shows cause and effect "without explanation" ([Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2025/11/04/read-play-working-with-system-dynamics-shapez-io-krystal-li/)). Note the current rules accept any placement geometrically and let a mis-oriented machine simply starve, so the reason strings must cover "legal but will not be fed" as a warning, not a refusal. *Accept:* e2e step places a crusher beside a hopper and asserts the warning text; unit test maps every `RunError` from `validateAction` to a non-empty player string; screenshot shows arrows on the ghost in both mouse and keyboard modes.

**0.3.3 Undo and redo in the build phase, covering rotations** — S/M. *What:* Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y (and on-screen buttons) over the build-phase action list up to the last run; undo of a rotation restores the previous facing; the replay stays a flat list of final actions (undo removes, it does not record). *Why:* missing undo, copy-paste and drag-select is the single largest Opus Magnum QoL complaint cluster (**0.42** and **0.22**) ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/558990/opus_magnum.md)); Factorio players filed bugs because undo did not reverse rotations ([Factorio forums](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?p=565669)); Parabox makes undo/redo/reset available from the first levels ([Tutorial Design of Patrick's Parabox](https://siddarthrg.substack.com/p/tutorial-design-of-patricks-parabox)). *Accept:* sim test: place→rotate→undo→undo yields the pre-place layout and the exported replay verifies; e2e presses Ctrl+Z after a rotate and asserts facing; keyboard-only path reaches undo.

**0.3.4 Tutorial seed: three constrained micro-rounds, then the real game** — M. *What:* a hand-authored seed (`tutorial-1`) whose first three rounds each exercise exactly one rule with offers constrained so the layout "can't easily mess up": round 1 offers Crusher only and the hint shows the single orientation that works; round 2 offers Furnace and teaches "behind only" again plus chaining; round 3 offers Splitter and teaches merging via conveyor side-load; rounds 4–6 are an ordinary draft. One guidance surface (the hint strip) carries the objective and is sufficient alone; no modal pauses except the first run start. Replaces the current first-run hints when the profile is new; offered as "Learn (3 min)" on the title afterwards. *Why:* Factorio's lessons — concepts in level one, teach through experimentation, unify information channels, objective sufficient if all bubbles are skipped ([Factorio FFF #241](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-241); [Factorio FFF #342](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-342)); the cautionary tale is Factorio's scrapped NPE, which changed the real progression to make teaching easier and was judged wrong at the foundation ([Factorio FFF #327](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-327)), so the tutorial must remain a genuine draft with the real rules. *Accept:* e2e drives a fresh profile through `tutorial-1` to round 4 in ≤ 40 scripted actions with no modal after the first; the autoplayer on `tutorial-1` reaches ingots by round 3 in 100% of tries; constrained offers are implemented as a tutorial-only offer table so `draftOffers` for every other seed is byte-identical (snapshot test).

**0.3.5 Live money heartbeat and stall indicator during the run** — S. *What:* the running total ticks up per shipped item with the existing value-scaled chime; when no item has shipped for 8 ticks and the hopper is blocked or a machine is jammed, the HUD shows a small "stalled" marker pointing at the first blocking cell (the data already exists in the live jam tint and `CellStats`). Respects reduced motion (text change only). *Why:* shapez.io's goal counter doubles as an efficiency readout and "stops when a clog blocks the system so cause and effect are visible" ([Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2025/11/04/read-play-working-with-system-dynamics-shapez-io-krystal-li/)); the inferred shapez frustration is clogs whose cause is not obvious. *Accept:* e2e runs a layout with a deliberate head-on conveyor and asserts the stall marker appears within 10 ticks and names the cell; perf tool shows no change in script ms/frame beyond noise.

**0.3.6 Dependency-gated tips with live mini-simulations** — M. *What:* a "Tips" panel reachable from the title and the build phase; each tip is a 2×3-cell live sim loop rendered with the real renderer (crusher orientation, side-loading merge, splitter alternation, furnace on raw scrap makes slag, press needs two shards, sell from bin); a tip is suggested once (non-modal badge) the first time its machine is drafted and marked read when opened. *Why:* Factorio replaced static screenshots with in-engine simulations because screenshots went stale and tied tips to dependencies and player actions ([Factorio FFF #361](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-361)); Game Accessibility Guidelines ask for contextual help and control reminders during gameplay ([GAG Intermediate](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/intermediate/)). *Accept:* each tip's loop is produced by `replay()` on a fixed mini-layout (unit-tested to ship the expected item); e2e: drafting a press for the first time shows the badge, opening marks it read and it does not reappear.

**0.3.7 Opt-in first-session funnel events** — S/M. *What:* a title-screen toggle "Share anonymous play statistics" (off by default, explained in one sentence with a link to the privacy page from 0.9.5); when on, the client batches a fixed event list (see Measurement plan) to `POST /events` on the verifier, which stores counts keyed by a random install id and the daily-salted IP hash it already uses for rate limiting, retaining raw events 30 days. *Why:* every onboarding decision in the research was made on designer judgement for lack of telemetry; funnel instrumentation ("only 13% ... got to complete it") is the standard way to find the step with the largest drop ([GameAnalytics: Exploring gaming funnels](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/exploring-gaming-funnels)); IP addresses and pseudonymous ids are personal data under UK GDPR, so consent and minimisation are required ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/personal-information-what-is-it/what-is-personal-information-a-guide/)). *Accept:* verifier test rejects events when the install has not consented; no event carries a seed string that is not `daily-*`/`tutorial-*` (layout privacy); a `tools/funnel.ts` report prints the step-conversion table from the stored data.

**Metrics to watch (v0.3).** From 0.3.1 recordings: median time to first run (target ≤ 3 min), rejected placements before the first valid one (target median ≤ 2). From 0.3.7 once hosted: `first_run_complete` / `app_start` ≥ 80%, `tutorial_complete` ≥ 70%, `second_game_start` ≥ 50% of first-run completers — targets synthesised from the case studies, not an industry benchmark. **Risks.** Over-constraining the tutorial into a different game (Factorio's NPE failure); fixing the playtest's loudest single voice instead of patterns; the hint strip becoming a second noisy channel if 0.3.2 and 0.3.6 both write to it (rule: rejections pre-empt, tips never interrupt).

### v0.4 — The run is the show (no rules change)

**Theme.** Every finished run produces three things worth looking at: a score placed on a distribution, a looping clip of the factory, and a spoiler-free text block. The juice pass is tied strictly to sim events and scaled by magnitude.

**0.4.1 Histogram-first results and daily strip** — M. *What:* after a run (and on the title's daily strip) show the distribution of verified scores for that seed as a histogram with the player's bar highlighted, percentile text ("better than 61% of 38 verified runs"), an "around you" slice of five above and five below, and the top five as today. With fewer than five verified entries, show the entries that exist plus the autoplayer par (0.4.5) and never a fabricated population. The verifier adds `GET /histogram?seed=` returning bin counts, N and the caller's percentile by replay hash. *Why:* histograms are what pushed Opus Magnum players to revisit solved puzzles ([Starrs Slog](https://starrslog.bearblog.dev/paul-opuses-magnum/)) and Infinifactory presents each metric as a histogram of all players plus friends ([Wikipedia: Infinifactory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinifactory)); a field experiment with over 1,000 users found contributions rose when peer scores were more dispersed and when average peer scores were lowered, suggesting comparison against the body of the distribution rather than the leader ([UMN Conservancy](https://conservancy.umn.edu/bitstreams/f4f85e78-b169-4d1e-9b21-533113feae28/download)); percentile displays are argued to be less demotivating than raw rank for mid-pack players ([Psychology of Games](https://www.psychologyofgames.com/2017/06/compared-to-other-heroes-of-the-storm/)). *Accept:* verifier unit test for bins and percentile with 1, 4 and 200 entries; e2e asserts the highlighted bar and percentile string after a submission to the test verifier; text sizes pass `tests/a11y.test.ts`.

**0.4.2 Loop export: WebM first, GIF second** — M then M. *What:* "Export clip" on the results screen re-simulates the chosen round (default: the highest-earning round) through the real renderer into an offscreen canvas at 4× and records 80 ticks via `canvas.captureStream()` + `MediaRecorder` to WebM (zero dependencies); a second increment bundles a small MIT GIF encoder for a GIF of the same frames at reduced size. Filename `junkyard-<seed>-$<score>-r<round>.webm`; the first and last frames carry seed and score as a caption; a share button copies the file where the clipboard API allows. Exports always render with full motion regardless of the reduced-motion setting (it is the viewer's choice). *Why:* GIF export is among the most-praised Opus Magnum features (**0.21**) and players embed the score triple in filenames by hand ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/558990/opus_magnum.md); [Starrs Slog](https://starrslog.bearblog.dev/paul-opuses-magnum/)); Zachtronics: "It's satisfying to watch factories work, and especially so when it's one of your own design" ([Game Developer: Opus Magnum](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-zachtronics-i-opus-magnum-i-)); the community required a GIF for records so "others immediately had access to the design and could replicate or improve it" ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/tracking-the-global-opus-magnum-records/)). *Accept:* e2e exports a WebM in Chromium and asserts a non-empty blob with duration ≥ 3 s; export time ≤ 10 s at 4× on the perf tool's reference layout; packaged Electron smoke test saves the file through the preload bridge.

**0.4.3 Spoiler-free share grid** — S. *What:* revise `share.ts` so the default share text is: date or seed label, score, percentile (if verified), days-played count, and one line of six glyphs (one per round) on fixed published thresholds of round earnings (e.g. ░ ≤ $100, ▒ ≤ $250, ▓ ≤ $400, █ above), with no URL by default and an optional toggle to append the permalink. Per-round dollar amounts move to a second "detailed" variant. *Why:* Wardle built the share feature after players typed emoji grids by hand, left the link out because it would look "trashy", and the grid "tells a story" while letting players "compare performance without spoilers" ([AP via Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/national-news/2022/01/04/he-made-wordle-for-his-partner-now-its-an-online-hit/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2256733); [BuzzFeed News](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/stefficao/how-wordle-went-viral-strategy)). *Accept:* `tests/share.test.ts` covers thresholds at boundaries, verified vs local wording, and that no layout data appears; glyphs chosen pass the CVD audit's shape-not-colour rule trivially (they are monochrome text).

**0.4.4 Juice pass bound to sim events** — M. *What:* an audit of every sim event (`ship`, `transform`, `absorb`, `jam`, `blocked`, round fanfare) against three rules: every event has one sound and at most one visual; intensity scales with value (ingot > plate > shard > scrap, already partly true for chimes); nothing shakes the screen or trails. Add the two missing beats the research names: a distinct draft "lock-in" sound and a per-round "all shipped" settle when the last item in flight clears. All gated by reduced motion as today. *Why:* LocalThunk spent "hundreds of hours on feel" and raised speed "until systems broke" ([Game Maker's Notebook transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/balatro-creator-localthunk-inspirations-jokers/)); Lovato: "All interactions the player can make should have a clear audio feedback" and fast effects "are only relevant in specific situations" ([Game Developer: Squeezing more juice](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/squeezing-more-juice-out-of-your-game-design-)). *Accept:* a table in `docs/DESIGN.md` listing event→sound→visual with no empty rows; perf tool unchanged within noise; reduced-motion e2e step still passes.

**0.4.5 Autoplayer par per seed** — S. *What:* the verifier runs the shipped autoplayer (120 tries, ~seconds) when it first sees a seed and stores the median as "bot par"; client shows "Bot par $1,697" on the results histogram and the daily strip, labelled as a bot, never ranked. *Why:* gives every seed a population-independent target on day one without inventing competitors (a `CLAUDE.md` rule) and mirrors Trackmania's medal targets that "give a solo goal independent of rank" ([TrueTrophies](https://i1.truetrophies.com/t595947/daily-trackmaster-trophy)). *Accept:* verifier test: par is deterministic for a seed across two processes; the autoplayer run is capped by wall-clock and executed in a worker so it cannot block submissions.

**0.4.6 Non-ranked run badges: Clean, Minimalist, Full Grid** — S. *What:* computed from the verified replay — Clean (no jam and no blocked hopper tick in any round), Minimalist (≤ N machines placed in round 6 while scoring ≥ bot par), Full Grid (all 48 cells used in a round). Shown on the results screen and in the detailed share variant; never a leaderboard. *Why:* Zachtronics gives "no in-game rewards for optimization" ([Game Developer: Opus Magnum](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-zachtronics-i-opus-magnum-i-)); Dorfromantik's challenges "encourage different play styles" via unlocks rather than ladders ([Game Developer: Dorfromantik](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/sparking-joy-through-tile-placement-in-idyllic-village-builder-i-dorfromantik-i-)); PC Gamer's "hidden third optimization" is aesthetics ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/perfectly-solving-opus-magnums-puzzles-is-impossible-but-thats-ok)). *Accept:* sim test computes badges from `RunResult`; badges are absent on unverified runs' share text.

**0.4.7 Host the verifier** — L ⚑. *What:* deploy `server/dist/verify.mjs` (or the Docker image) on a small host with TLS, `CORS_ORIGIN` set to the web build's origin, `DATA_DIR` on persistent storage, and bake `VERIFIER_URL` into the web and desktop builds. *Why:* every feature from 0.4.1 onward is only observable with a population; the service already supports host, port, rate limit, request log and health. *Accept:* `/health` reachable from the web build; the e2e daily submission passes against the hosted URL in a manual check recorded in STATUS.

**Metrics to watch (v0.4).** Share of finished runs that export a clip or copy share text (target ≥ 15% once hosted); share of submitters who open the histogram's "around you" slice; first-run completion must not drop after the juice pass. **Risks.** WebM/MediaRecorder support varies (Safari in particular — not a v0.4 target); histogram with N < 5 looks empty, which is why bot par ships in the same milestone; juice regressions for reduced-motion users (the existing e2e step guards it).

### v0.5 — Decisions (RULES_VERSION 2 → 3)

**Theme.** One new machine that only earns its slot because five materials are in flight, a paid reroll from the one wallet that is also the score, and a round-tiered offer pool — each admitted or rejected by `tools/balance.ts` on identical offers, exactly as the press was.

**0.5.1 Sieve (filter): raw scrap forward, everything else turns right** — M. *What:* a 1-cell machine, input from behind, no hold; raw scrap exits forward, any processed material (shards, plate, ingot, slag) exits to the right side (orientation picks which side); if the chosen exit is blocked the item waits (jam-on-full, so it is a visible decision versus the overflow-style splitter). Draft weight ~10, offered from round 3 (0.5.3). *Why:* a filter "only earns its slot if there are ≥2 item kinds in flight" and Satisfactory's Smart Splitters exist so "mixed belts can be sorted" ([Alt-F4 #45](https://alt-f4.blog/ALTF4-45/)); the concrete decision it creates is protecting a furnace from raw scrap (which makes $1 slag) while recycling the scrap through a crusher — a routing detour that spends cells, which is the Kaizen-style trade-off ([Slant Magazine](https://www.slantmagazine.com/games/kaizen-a-factory-story-review)). Having both a blocking and an overflow router reproduces the force-vs-priority choice Factorio players asked for ([Factorio forums](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?p=352785)). *Accept:* sim tests for both exits, blocked-exit wait and pass-through of each material; `tools/balance.ts` over 40 seeds × 120 tries: picked in 25–50% of rounds it is offered, **0 seeds worse** when allowed vs banned, mean lift > 0; autoplayer sweep finds no score above the sanity bound; card art, badge strings and a tip (0.3.6) ship together.

**0.5.2 Reroll the offer, paid from the running score** — S/M. *What:* once per round the player may reroll the three offers for a fixed cost (start at $40, roughly one starter round) deducted from the running total; refused if the total is below the cost; the new offers come from the independent stream `${seed}:draft:${round}:reroll:1`, so later rounds never change; a new rule-checked action `reroll` enters the replay schema and the autoplayer learns to use it. *Why:* LocalThunk deleted Balatro's separate reroll currency because it "just plain sucked" and the game improved ([Game Maker's Notebook transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/balatro-creator-localthunk-inspirations-jokers/)); per-purpose RNG streams keyed by purpose (never offset) are what keep seeded runs stable so that an optional action "shouldn't change the next shop's contents" ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2379780/discussions/0/715609751875021265); [tck.mn](https://tck.mn/blog/correlated-randomness-sts2/)); the existing `${seed}:draft:${round}` keying already follows this pattern. *Accept:* sim test: rerolling in round 2 leaves rounds 3–6 offers identical to a non-reroll run on the same seed; replay with `reroll` verifies and an unaffordable reroll is rejected as an illegal action; balance tool: autoplayer rerolls in 10–30% of rounds (LocalThunk's 15–25% skip-rate target as the analogue) and no seed's best drops.

**0.5.3 Round-tiered offer pool** — S. *What:* rounds 1–2 draw from the seven 0.2.0 machines (round 1 still guarantees a Crusher); the Sieve and any future "advanced" machine join the pool from round 3; the tier table lives in `rules.ts` and is identical for every player on a seed. *Why:* Super Auto Pets adds a shop tier every two turns and Luck be a Landlord offers only Common symbols on the first spins, borrowing Magic's "New World Order" ([Two Average Gamers](https://www.twoaveragegamers.com/ultimate-guide-to-super-auto-pets-game-mechanics/); [TrampolineTales Q&A](https://blog.trampolinetales.com/questions-and-danswers/)); it is both onboarding and late-run escalation, and because it depends only on (seed, round) it is seed-fair. *Accept:* snapshot test of offers for 50 seeds × 6 rounds; the 0.2.0 seven are never excluded from any round.

**0.5.4 Tie-breaker on the verified board** — S. *What:* equal scores rank by fewer machines placed in round 6, then by earlier server receipt; the rule is printed under every board. *Why:* the early Opus Magnum cycle board broke ties by cost then area, and secondary metrics became the way to separate solutions once minimums were common ([Biggie Blog](https://biggieblog.com/?p=836)); this keeps one ranked axis (the self-doubt finding) while making near-optimal seeds decidable. *Accept:* verifier unit test on three equal-score entries.

**0.5.5 Balance and exploit sweep as the gate** — S (recurring). *What:* re-run `tools/autoplay.ts` (20 seeds × 120) and `tools/balance.ts` after 0.5.1–0.5.3, store `docs/baselines/0.3.0/`, update DESIGN's decisions log with the numbers, and only then bump to v3. *Why:* Mega Crit's warning that "metrics can be misleading if not carefully interpreted" ([Podwise summary](https://podwise.ai/episodes/1459444)) is why the gate uses identical-offer comparison rather than raw score drift; the press decision in `docs/STATUS.md` is the precedent. *Accept:* baseline JSON files committed; dead-end seeds (no improving draft in any round) ≤ 2 of 20 as today.

**Metrics to watch (v0.5).** Sieve pick rate when offered (25–50%), reroll rate per round (10–30%), distinct machine-set signatures among the top 20% of verified runs per daily (a rough "viable builds" count — target ≥ 3 distinct signatures on most seeds), dead-end seed count. **Risks.** The Sieve becomes a trap for greedy drafters as plate $9/$10 did for the press — the balance tool's "0 seeds worse" criterion is the guard; the reroll cost is wrong in one direction (too cheap makes it reroll-fishing, too dear makes it dead) — tune by the autoplayer's usage band before human play; replays and saves from v2 are invalidated, so announce the bump on the title for one release.

### v0.6 — The daily, done right (no rules change; verifier protocol change)

**Theme.** One public UTC seed minted by the server, a 48-hour window with one deadline for everyone, best verified score per player, an archive where nothing is ever lost, and a streak that counts days played and forgives.

**0.6.1 Stable player identity without passwords** — M. *What:* on first launch the client generates a random 128-bit player token (stored in `jd.settings.v1` / Electron userData) and the verifier maps `player_id → display_name`, keying boards by `(seed, player_id)` with `KeepBest` semantics; names stay non-unique labels but gain a four-character tag derived from the token hash for disambiguation; a one-click "export recovery code" and "restore" on the title; the existing name prompt and `jd.submissions.v1` migrate. *Why:* the zero-friction ladder is device token now, Steam ticket later, hosted auth later still, and the device token "gives stable ranks and name ownership with zero sign-up UX" at the cost of no cross-device sync ([Steamworks: User Authentication](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/auth) for the later rung); one entry per identity per board is Steam's own model ([Steamworks: Leaderboards](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/leaderboards)); Among Us kept names non-exclusive ("Multiple people can have the same one") and gated changes behind accounts ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/among-us-account-system-safety-harassment-kids-185830761.html)). *Accept:* verifier test: two submissions from one `player_id` on one seed keep the better; a restored recovery code reclaims the name and rank; names pass the blocklist from 0.9.3 once it exists (stub now).

**0.6.2 Server-minted daily seed with commitment, client formula kept for practice** — M. *What:* the verifier computes `seed = "daily-" + date + "-" + base32(HMAC(secret, date))[0:8]` and serves it from `GET /daily` only once 00:00 UTC has passed, together with `SHA-256(tomorrow's seed)` as a commitment published a day ahead and verifiable the next day; ranked submissions must use a server-issued seed for that date; the client-derivable `daily-YYYY-MM-DD` remains the offline/unranked practice seed and is labelled as such; the ruleset/build hash is pinned per day so a mid-window patch cannot change outcomes. *Why:* the seed cannot be hidden from a client that generates offers, so fairness rests on it being unknowable before the window opens — Wordle's client shipped "the answer to every puzzle, past, present, and future" until the NYT moved answers server-side ([ITV/PrimalScreen](https://primalscreen.itvmedia.co.uk/tiktoker-ruins-wordle-by-revealing-future-answers-in-site-code.html); [sbplat/wordle](https://github.com/sbplat/wordle)); AntGame.io binds server-issued seeds to the submitter and rejects mismatches ([AntGame.io README](https://github.com/Cuzzo01/antgame.io)); publishing a hash before and the preimage after is the provably-fair pattern, with the documented limit that the operator still chooses the seed ([Medium](https://medium.com/@rishi_36645/why-server-seed-commitments-arent-fully-trust-less-647e13ab1c91)). The autoplayer shipping with the game makes precomputation a real threat here, not a theoretical one. *Accept:* verifier test: `/daily` before 00:00 UTC returns yesterday's seed; a submission for a date's seed received before that seed's issue time is rejected with `outside_window`; the commitment verifies against the revealed seed; `daily.ts` tests updated and the e2e daily step passes against the test verifier's clock.

**0.6.3 48-hour window, frozen boards, archive** — M. *What:* day D accepts submissions until D+2 00:00 UTC and then freezes; the title shows the real remaining time only ("closes in 31 h"), never urgency copy; every past daily is listed in an archive and playable as practice with its frozen histogram and replays; the verifier keeps replays for the top 100 per seed as today and the score list for all. *Why:* Trackmania uses one global release instant and reruns for other regions; Chess.com gives 48 hours and a calendar for catch-up; Wordle's local-midnight reset leaks answers across zones and breaks travellers' streaks ([Trackmania docs](https://doc.trackmania.com/play/what-is-totd/); [Chess.com support](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8708990-how-does-the-daily-puzzle-work); [TechRadar column](https://techradar.com/opinion/my-wordle-streak-just-hit-500-heres-what-ive-learned)); an archive means skipping a day costs nothing beyond that day's rank, which is the temporal dark-pattern line ([Gigazine on darkpattern.games](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20251117-dark-pattern-game)). *Accept:* verifier test for acceptance at D+1 23:59 and rejection at D+2 00:01 with reason code; archive e2e opens a past day, plays, and confirms nothing is submitted.

**0.6.4 Days-played streak with grace, displayed beside total days** — S. *What:* streak = consecutive UTC days with a verified submission (the cheapest qualifying action), extended if the submission lands within the day's 48-hour window; display "17 days played · 5 in a row"; a break shows the new count with no loss message, no red, no notification; reminders exist only as an opt-in, user-timed, neutrally worded desktop notification (off by default). *Why:* decoupling the streak from the daily goal so that one lesson extends it lifted Duolingo's D14 retention 3.3% and the share of learners on a streak 10.5% ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)); learners with a 7-day streak were 2.4× likelier to return next day ([same](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)); broken streaks reduce engagement most when users blame themselves and least when repair is possible ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://udspace.udel.edu/handle/19716/34160)); Duolingo's guilt-framed reminders were called "20 days of emotional blackmail" ([UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/20-days-of-emotional-blackmail-from-duolingo-4f566523e3c5)). *Accept:* unit tests for grace at the window boundary; the UI string set contains no loss/urgency wording (lint list); notification defaults off and is absent on web.

**0.6.5 Medals after close** — S. *What:* when a day freezes, the verifier computes bronze/silver/gold thresholds from the verified distribution (e.g. 50th/80th/95th percentile, published) and the client shows a medal on the archive entry and in the detailed share variant; bot par remains the day-of target. *Why:* medal targets give "a solo goal independent of rank" ([TrueTrophies](https://i1.truetrophies.com/t595947/daily-trackmaster-trophy)) and Trackmania's trophy table pays far down the board so the long tail feels rewarded ([Trackmania docs](https://doc.trackmania.com/play/what-is-totd/)); a dissertation experiment found tier-based leaderboards raised motivation for high and low placers alike ([WSU dissertation](https://rex.libraries.wsu.edu/esploro/outputs/doctoral/LEVELING-THE-COMPETITION-THE-IMPACT-OF/99901221152701842)). *Accept:* verifier test on a synthetic distribution; medals never shown before freeze.

**0.6.6 Published fairness and verification policy page** — S. *What:* a static page (and in-game panel) stating seed derivation, the commitment scheme, the attempt policy, tie-breakers, what "verified" means, reason codes, retention, and the appeal route. *Why:* transparency is one of the fairness rules the daily-challenge research converges on, and Trackmania's opaque report flow is the counter-example ([doc.trackmania.com](https://doc.trackmania.com/general/how-to-report-player/)). *Accept:* page linked from the title and the results screen; its text is generated from the same constants `rules.ts` and the verifier use (no drift).

**Metrics to watch (v0.6).** Share of daily players who submit at least one verified run (target ≥ 60% of those who finish a daily run), median attempts per submitter (expect 1–3; a long tail indicates grinding without a rank incentive, which is fine), 7-day distribution of days played, weekend dip (Duolingo saw 5–10% ([Duolingo blog, streaks](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals))). **Risks.** The server-minted seed makes the ranked daily depend on the verifier being up at 00:00 UTC — fallback is the labelled practice seed; identity tokens are lost with cleared browser storage — the recovery code mitigates, and Steam binding later removes it; the 48-hour window delays "final" boards by a day, so the UI must say "provisional" until freeze.

### v0.7 — Play with people (no rules change)

**Theme.** Every verified run becomes something another person can race, watch, fork or beat — without anyone needing to be online at the same time.

**0.7.1 Challenge link ("beat this")** — M. *What:* any verified entry exports `jd://challenge?seed=…&entry=<hash>` (and a web URL); the recipient plays the same seed with the challenger's per-round earnings overlaid live (the replay viewer already re-simulates "Original run: $X this round"); results land on a private two-row board and in a share line; practice and challenge play on a daily seed inside its window still count toward the daily board through the normal verified path. *Why:* Balatro communities already run "beat my seed" threads by hand ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2379780/discussions/0/604143386282226837)); Trackmania lets players race the ghosts "surrounding your current ranking" ([doc.trackmania.com](https://doc.trackmania.com/play/watch-replays/)); the format needs no critical mass. *Accept:* e2e: open a challenge link, see the overlay from round 1, finish, see the two-row result; the recipient's replay verifies independently.

**0.7.2 Groups: a code that scopes the board** — M. *What:* a player creates a group code (8 chars) and shares it; the daily strip, histogram and board gain a "Group: <name>" filter showing only members' verified entries; membership is a list on the verifier keyed by `player_id`; no chat. *Why:* friends-first boards are the fallback even a large studio chose when global boards became untrustworthy ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2868840/discussions/0/798966745989725135/)); Steam supports a "Reads: Friends" board setting natively, which this emulates for the web ([Steamworks: Leaderboards](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/leaderboards)); Super Auto Pets' private rooms are joined by a host code ([SAP wiki](https://superautopets.wiki.gg/wiki/The_Basics)). *Accept:* verifier tests for join/leave and filtered histogram; group names pass the name filter.

**0.7.3 Fork from round N into practice** — S/M. *What:* from any watched replay (verified or own), "Continue from round 3" loads the layout and bin state after round N into an unranked practice run on the same seed, labelled "forked from <name>"; forks can never be submitted. *Why:* Opus Magnum's community treated others' solutions as material to "replicate or improve"; Slay the Spire players value seeing "literally any player's deck and what they did in every fight" ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/tracking-the-global-opus-magnum-records/); [Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/1743357605566110144)); keeping forks unranked avoids copied solutions clogging boards. *Accept:* sim test: fork state equals the replay's state after round N; `act()` refuses `submit`-path in fork mode as it does in playback.

**0.7.4 Weekly seed with an announced modifier** — M. *What:* a seed released Friday 18:00 UTC with a 7-day window and one deterministic modifier chosen from a small published list (e.g. "hopper emits every 3 ticks", "no splitters offered", "docks pay double for plates"), announced at the start so the whole run is planned around it; its own board and histogram; modifiers are rule *parameters* already guarded by `RULES_VERSION` and are stored with the seed so replays verify. *Why:* Super Auto Pets' weekly pack rotation and Opus Magnum's weekly tournaments with bespoke metrics sustain mastery without fragmenting the main ladder ([Pocket Gamer](https://ee.pocketgamer.com/articles/084151/super-auto-pets-review-chill-auto-chess-game-with-deep-strategic-gameplay/); [Biggie Blog: MechA](https://biggieblog.com/?p=1521)); Into the Breach's ending "should be a culmination of knowledge", not a surprise ([Game Developer: Subset Games](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-subset-games-made-the-jump-from-i-ftl-i-to-i-into-the-breach-i-)); a Friday release covers the weekend dip. *Accept:* sim tests per modifier; replay files carry the modifier id and the verifier rejects a mismatch; the autoplayer sweep on each modifier finds no exploit.

**0.7.5 Discord webhook from the verifier** — S. *What:* on each new daily seed and on a new #1 (or new freeze), the verifier POSTs a short message to a configured webhook URL (one daily thread, one weekly thread); no bot hosting. *Why:* "Fifteen minutes daily of genuine presence outperforms any bot" and servers should start with 4–5 channels ([Stray Spark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/discord-community-building-game-dev); [Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-set-up-a-discord-server-for-your-game)); Spelunky's daily created a streaming ritual strong enough that the developer added a "playing the Daily" indicator ([PCGamesN](https://pcgamesn.com/indie/spelunky-update-whips-windows-8-compatibility-shape-bops-bugs)). *Accept:* verifier test with a mocked webhook; rate-limited to one message per event type per seed.

**0.7.6 Reveal other players' daily replays only after you submit or the day closes** — S. *What:* the "Watch #1" and board replay buttons on an open daily are disabled until the viewer has a verified entry for that day (or the day froze); archive days are always watchable. *Why:* Bénard's fairness leak under unlimited attempts is "watching a video and learning the dungeon by heart" ([Game Developer: The 24-hour ticket](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-24-hour-ticket-examining-daily-runs-)). *Accept:* e2e asserts the disabled state before and enabled state after a submission.

**Metrics to watch (v0.7).** Challenge links opened per link created; share of submitters in at least one group; forks started per watched replay; weekly participation vs daily. **Risks.** Groups without moderation become the first place abusive names appear — 0.9.3's filter must precede public promotion; modifiers multiply balance surface — keep the list to three at launch and add one per month; the replay-reveal gate frustrates lurkers, so the archive must be prominent.

### v0.8 — Everywhere it should run (no rules change)

**Theme.** Determinism proven across engines, layout and input ready for a handheld and a controller, a screen-reader path, strings externalised, the verifier hardened and the Steam plumbing in place — all before the beta brings strangers.

**0.8.1 Cross-engine golden replays in CI** — S/M. *What:* a corpus of 50 replays (autoplayer best per seed, tutorial, each modifier) with recorded `hash(event stream)` and `hash(final state)`; a Playwright test runs the sim in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit and compares to Node's hashes; an ESLint rule (or grep test) forbids `Math.random|sin|cos|tan|exp|pow|log`, `Date`, `Intl`, `toLocale*` and comparator-less `sort` inside `game/src/sim/`. *Why:* a 2026 rigid-body case produced different state hashes on Node 20 and Chrome 149 until `sin/cos` were replaced with an fdlibm port, while `Math.sqrt` (IEEE-required) matched ([perplexdotgg/bounce issue #8](https://codeberg.org/perplexdotgg/bounce/issues/8)); Rune patches ~31 Math functions and replaces `Math.random` with seeded mulberry32 to keep JS deterministic ([Rune](https://developers.rune.ai/blog/making-js-deterministic-for-fun-and-glory/)). The sim already uses mulberry32 and integer ops, so this is proof rather than repair. *Accept:* CI green on all three engines; the lint test fails on an injected `Math.sin`.

**0.8.2 Scalable canvas and a handheld preset** — M. *What:* keep the 1120×700 logical layout but scale the canvas to fit the window (integer scale where possible, letterboxed otherwise), add a "Handheld" preset that forces text size ≥ 12 px at 1280×800 and larger hit targets. *Why:* Steam Deck Verified wants 1280×800, "the smallest on-screen font character should never fall below 9 pixels in height" with 12 px recommended and user-configurable text size ([Steamworks: Steam Deck compatibility](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/steamdeck/compat)); XAG 101 asks for 18 px body text at 1080p on PC, resizable to 200% ([XAG 101](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/101)); `ROADMAP.md` already lists the fixed layout as the open scaling item. *Accept:* e2e at 1280×800 and 1920×1080 viewports asserts no clipping and the smallest glyph ≥ 12 px in the preset; `tests/a11y.test.ts` extended with the preset's font sizes.

**0.8.3 Gamepad support over the keyboard cursor** — M. *What:* Gamepad API mapping d-pad/left stick to the existing grid cursor, face buttons to place/pick-up, rotate, return-to-bin and run, shoulder buttons to cycle bin parts and speed; on-screen prompts switch glyph set (keyboard/Xbox-style) by last input; no text entry needed for play (name entry stays optional). *Why:* Deck Verified requires that "Players must not need to adjust any in-game settings in order to enable controller support" and that glyphs match the active input ([Steamworks: Steam Deck compatibility](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/steamdeck/compat)); XAG 107: all UI navigable with single, non-simultaneous presses, activation on release with a cancel path ([XAG 107](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/107)). *Accept:* e2e injects gamepad events through Playwright's CDP and completes a round; glyph switch screenshot; the keyboard e2e still passes.

**0.8.4 Screen-reader mirror: roving-tabindex grid plus live regions** — M/L. *What:* a visually hidden DOM beside the canvas: a 48-cell grid with roving tabindex where each cell is labelled ("row 3, column 2: Furnace, facing east, input from west, fed"), the offers as a listbox, bin as a list, one polite live region for state ("Placed Crusher at C3 facing east") and one assertive region for rejections (reusing 0.3.2's strings), and the run reported as a summarised log per round. *Why:* a canvas is "a black box to a screen reader", the practical pattern is a parallel accessible DOM with announcements ([bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-fix-web-game-canvas-no-screen-reader-announcements)); the APG grid pattern and `aria-live` semantics are specified ([W3C APG](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/example-index/grid/dataGrids.html); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Guides/Live_regions)); the game is turn-based and keyboard placement exists, so "the delta is mainly labelling and announcements". *Accept:* Playwright accessibility snapshot lists all 48 cells with labels; an e2e step completes a round using only the mirror; at least one tester who uses a screen reader is recruited for 0.9.1 (GAG: "Include some players with impairments in playtesting" ([GAG Intermediate](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/intermediate/))).

**0.8.5 Full key remapping and documented controls** — S. *What:* a settings panel to rebind every action (place, rotate, pick up, return, cycle, run, speed, undo, redo, pause), persisted, with hints and tutorial text reading the current binding. *Why:* GAG Basic: "Allow controls to be remapped / reconfigured"; Opus Magnum complaint: controls undocumented or unrebindable (**0.16**) ([GAG Basic](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/basic/); [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/558990/opus_magnum.md)). *Accept:* e2e rebinds rotate and asserts the hint text and behaviour.

**0.8.6 Externalised strings and a pseudo-locale** — M. *What:* every user-visible string moves to one keyed table with placeholders (no concatenation for numbers); a debug pseudo-locale lengthens by 30–50% with accented glyphs and brackets; layout containers wrap or ellipsise; a glyph-coverage check for the font stack. No translations yet. *Why:* localisation decisions should wait for wishlist data by country, but the codebase work is cheap now and expensive later; Godot's pseudolocalisation guidance treats 30% expansion as adequate ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/i18n/pseudolocalization.html)); Simplified Chinese made China the #1 revenue region for one indie ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/china-is-kind-of-a-big-deal-for-indie-games)); a ~2,500-word systems game costs roughly $80–150 per European language per a vendor estimate ([Alconost](https://alconost.com/en/blog/steam-language-mix-indies)). *Accept:* a test fails on any string literal rendered outside the table; pseudo-locale screenshot shows no clipping on title, build, results.

**0.8.7 Verifier hardening and storage migration** — M. *What:* body-size cap on every route, action-count and tick caps from `rules.ts`, each verification in a worker with a wall-clock timeout, per-player submission bucket (start at 2/min), atomic file writes, then migration from the JSON file to SQLite (WAL) with a one-shot importer; reason codes `did_not_reproduce | rate_limited | invalid_seed | outside_window | too_large` returned to the client. *Why:* OWASP API4:2023 lists exactly these controls — payload sizes, array lengths, execution timeouts, per-client rate limits ([OWASP API4](https://api-security.owasp.org/editions/2023/en/0xa4-unrestricted-resource-consumption)); AntGame.io uses 2 submissions/min/user ([AntGame.io README](https://github.com/Cuzzo01/antgame.io)); SQLite "competes with fopen()" and serves sites under ~100K hits/day ([SQLite: Appropriate Uses](https://www.sqlite.org/whentouse.html)). *Accept:* tests for each reason code and the timeout; importer round-trips the existing `DATA_DIR`; `/health` unchanged.

**0.8.8 Steam plumbing behind a build flag** — L ⚑. *What:* a Steam-only Electron build (separate from the web build so the web keeps a strict CSP) that uses `steamworks.js` for achievements, rich presence, Auto-Cloud and `isSteamRunningOnSteamDeck`; the verifier validates `GetAuthTicketForWebApi` tickets via `AuthenticateUserTicket`, binds the SteamID to the `player_id`, and writes verified scores to a trusted-write Steam leaderboard via `SetLeaderboardScore`. Requires the Steamworks app fee and a publisher key (human). *Why:* `steamworks.js` has no leaderboard namespace and needs `contextIsolation: false` ([steamworks.js README](https://github.com/ceifa/steamworks.js); [client.d.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ceifa/steamworks.js/main/client.d.ts)); `onlytrustedwrites=true` means "scores cannot be set by clients" ([Steamworks ISteamLeaderboards](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/webapi/ISteamLeaderboards)), which turns the existing verifier into the anti-cheat story; Auto-Cloud needs no code ([Steamworks Cloud](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/cloud)); Phaser documents Electron + electron-builder + Steamworks upload as the route ([Phaser](https://phaser.io/news/2025/03/publishing-web-games-on-steam-with-electron)). *Accept:* a mocked-Steam unit test for the ticket→SteamID→board write path; the web build's bundle contains no Steam code (size check).

**Metrics to watch (v0.8).** CI pass rate across engines; `did_not_reproduce` count with otherwise-valid replays (a determinism bug signal, logged separately from rejections); install→first run on the handheld preset. **Risks.** Firefox/WebKit expose a determinism bug in rendering-independent code (unlikely given the audit, but this is the milestone to find out); the Steam build's relaxed Electron security must never leak into the web build; SQLite migration touches the one piece of state that cannot be lost — rehearse on a copy of production data.

### v0.9 — Beta: integrity operations and the funnel (RULES_VERSION frozen for 1.0)

**Theme.** Strangers arrive through a Steam Playtest or an open web beta; the boards survive it; the first-session funnel is measured and fixed; rules are frozen so every replay from here verifies at 1.0.

**0.9.1 Playtest round two and a Steam Playtest** — L ⚑. *What:* repeat the 0.3.1 protocol with 4–5 fresh testers on the tutorial plus two dailies, including at least one screen-reader user and one controller-only user; in parallel open a Steam Playtest (or a password itch build) for scale, read only the funnel data from it. *Why:* Steam Playtest is a separate child app, free, with batch or open signup, and its reviews, wishlists and refunds do not touch the main page ([Steamworks: Playtest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest)); remote testers "won't report failures" so recorded think-aloud remains the design instrument ([gtstu](https://gtstu.com/playtest-indie-game-solo-developer/)). *Accept:* `docs/PLAYTEST.md` round two; funnel report shows the targets from the Measurement plan or names the step being fixed.

**0.9.2 Human and Assisted boards** — M. *What:* the autoplayer and any `window.__jd` dev-API submission tag the replay `origin: "tool"`; tagged entries go to an "Assisted" board shown with equal prominence; the main board is "Human" by self-declaration; moderators can reclassify with a logged reason; identical normalised action hashes from different `player_id`s on one seed are flagged (not removed) for review. *Why:* Trackmania TAS runs live on a separate spreadsheet and the 2021 scandal was found statistically, not cryptographically ([TASVideos](https://tasvideos.org/Forum/Topics/22766); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/cheating-allegations-catch-up-with-some-of-trackmanias-fastest-drivers)); the Opus Magnum community de-named its bot-verified frontier to make it "a collaborative search rather than a competition" ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/tracking-the-global-opus-magnum-records/)); publishing both boards makes assisted play "a feature, not a penalty bin". *Accept:* autoplayer submissions land on Assisted in a verifier test; reclassification writes an audit row; the duplicate-hash flag fires on two identical replays from two ids.

**0.9.3 Name moderation, report flow and appeals with outcome feedback** — M. *What:* server-side blocklist with Unicode-confusable and leetspeak normalisation at claim time; reserved strings that impersonate the developer; a rename cooldown; a "report name / report entry" button that hides the item pending review; a moderator action log; every rejection and reclassification carries a reason code the player can see, and a public appeal address. *Why:* Playdate screens names automatically then moderates manually and removes names that "pretend to be someone or something they're not" ([Playdate Community Expectations](https://help.play.date/manual/community-expectations/)); "a surprising number of moderation failures are not filter weaknesses but fields that were added later and never wired into the moderation pipeline" ([bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/qa-testing-for-user-generated-content-moderation)) — group names (0.7.2) and challenge labels must pass the same filter; speedrun.com allows disputes against any run and expects impartial moderators ([DNAbyss rules](https://www.speedrun.com/dnabyss/guides/8p9ai)). *Accept:* filter tests for leetspeak and confusables; every user-writable field enumerated in one test that pushes a blocked string through each.

**0.9.4 Anomaly signals recorded, never auto-banned** — S. *What:* the verifier records per entry: server receipt time vs seed issue time, submissions per identity per seed, client-reported think time per action (untrusted, informational), and hash collisions; a `tools/review.ts` lists outliers for a human. *Why:* Trackmania's lesson is to keep raw replays so "later forensic analysis is possible" and to flag statistically anomalous cadence for review rather than auto-ban ([Tildes](https://tildes.net/~games/x1x/the_biggest_cheating_scandal_in_trackmania_history)); turn-based play removes the slowdown-cheat class entirely. *Accept:* the review tool runs against the test fixture and lists the planted outlier.

**0.9.5 Privacy notice, data deletion, retention** — S ⚑ (owner writes the text). *What:* one hosted page and in-game panel: controller and contact, what is stored (name, player id, replays, scores, daily-salted IP hash, timestamps, consented events), purposes, lawful bases, retention (raw IP hashes 7 days, events 30 days, replays per the top-100 rule, scores until deletion), recipients (host, Steam if used), rights and the complaint route; a "delete my entries" request path; no email or birthdate collected on the web build. *Why:* UK GDPR treats IP addresses and pseudonymous ids as personal data and lists the notice contents ([ICO: What privacy information](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/the-right-to-be-informed/what-privacy-information-should-we-provide/)); network security is a recognised legitimate interest subject to the three-part test ([ICO: legitimate interests](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/legitimate-interests/when-can-we-rely-on-legitimate-interests/)); CCPA thresholds (≈$26.6M revenue or 100k consumers) will not apply ([Bastion](https://bastion.tech/learn/ccpa/who-needs-ccpa-compliance)). *Accept:* the page is linked from title, results and the consent toggle; deletion removes the name and replays and leaves an anonymised score (documented).

**0.9.6 Steam page, demo and the one Next Fest** — L ⚑. *What:* store page live with a trailer cut from exported clips (0.4.2), tags Automation / Strategy / Roguelite / Score Attack rather than Puzzle, a demo that is the tutorial plus the daily with local best only, submitted ≥ 5 weeks before the chosen fest; demo left up afterwards. *Why:* pre-fest wishlists predict fest gains (**Spearman r = 0.825**) so the page must collect for months first ([Cinevva summary of HTMAG](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/steam-next-fest-strategy-gamedeveloper)); a title may enter one Next Fest ever, builds go in 5 weeks early for Press Preview, and Valve encourages leaving the demo up ([Steamworks Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)); puzzle is among the weakest Steam genres while "crafty-buildy" strategy topped the Feb 2025 demo charts ([HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/04/steam-next-fest-feb-2025/)); an over-generous demo converted poorly in one case study ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/tales-from-discoverabilityland-when)). *Accept:* page live; demo build passes the packaged smoke test; wishlist count recorded weekly in STATUS.

**0.9.7 Content and rules freeze** — S. *What:* `RULES_VERSION` frozen; the balance baseline for the frozen version committed; the weekly modifier list fixed at three; `docs/DESIGN.md` decisions log updated. *Why:* Mini Motorways moved challenge definitions server-side so fixes ship without client updates ([Dinosaur Polo Club](https://dinopoloclub.com/2023/05/04/buckle-up-for-new-daily-challenges-with-the-extra-for-experts-update/)); here the equivalent is to stop changing rules so every beta replay stays valid at 1.0. *Accept:* a test asserts `RULES_VERSION` equals the frozen constant until a dated exemption.

**Metrics to watch (v0.9).** Funnel targets (Measurement plan); daily submitters per day and the share from the Playtest; flagged/reviewed/reclassified counts; wishlists per week. **Risks.** The beta exposes a board exploit — the frozen rules allow a verifier-side fix only if the exploit is a verifier bug, otherwise it waits for 1.0.1 with a bump and an announced void; moderation load lands on one person — the report-then-hide default keeps the board clean while reviews queue; Next Fest timing slips past 1.0 (Valve forbids releasing before the fest ends).

### v1.0 — Launch

**Theme.** Ship what the beta proved, with the launch mechanics the platform rewards, and a cadence plan that does not exhaust one developer.

**1.0.1 Signed Windows installer and the macOS decision** — M ⚑. *What:* sign the NSIS installer (Azure Artifact Signing at roughly $9.99/month for US/Canada individuals is the cheapest documented path; OV certificates $150–300/yr) ([West Wind blog](https://weblog.west-wind.com/posts/2025/Jul/20/Fighting-through-Setting-up-Microsoft-Trusted-Signing); [Microsoft code-signing options](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/package-and-deploy/code-signing-options)); macOS only if demand appears, since notarization requires the $99/yr program ([electron-builder notarization docs](https://www.electron.build/docs/notarization)); Steam's own updater replaces any auto-update code on that channel. *Accept:* installer runs on a clean Windows machine without the SmartScreen "unknown publisher" block (reputation may still warn initially); `tests/desktop-smoke.test.mjs` passes on the signed build.

**1.0.2 Launch pricing and discount calendar** — S ⚑. *What:* $9.99–$14.99 (the research's defensible band), a launch discount of at most 40% for 7–14 days set before release, no further discount for 30 days, seasonal sales thereafter. *Why:* GameDiscoverCo's median first-week conversion is ~0.15 sales per wishlist, falling to ~0.10 above $10, and "you are not going to sell twice as many copies ... at $10 versus $20" ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-the-state-of-steam); [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/why-you-may-be-underpricing-your)); Steam's discount rules are explicit ([Steamworks discounts](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/discounts)). *Accept:* the calendar is written in STATUS before release day.

**1.0.3 Review threshold as the launch KPI** — S. *What:* a title-screen "Enjoying it? A review helps" link shown once after the third finished daily (never on the results screen, never repeating), targeting **≥ 50 reviews within 30 days**. *Why:* no score shows below 10 reviews and "Very Positive" needs 50 at 80%+ (reverse-engineered thresholds) ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/steam-review-score-thresholds-explained)); 48.7% of 2025 Steam releases ended the year under 10 reviews ([MP1st / SteamDB](https://mp1st.com/news/18k-games-released-steam-2025-half-have-10-reviews)). *Accept:* the prompt fires once per install in e2e; review count logged weekly.

**1.0.4 Post-launch cadence** — S. *What:* one Major Update event every 6–10 weeks paired with a Steam Update Visibility Round, each adding at most one rule change behind a `RULES_VERSION` bump (candidates: a second advanced machine such as a value-multiplying "Stamp" if the balance tool shows furnace dominance, a fourth weekly modifier); weekly challenge posts stay on Discord and the web, not Steam announcements. *Why:* Valve: "If you don't have a reason to post an announcement, don't post one" and major updates should pair with a visibility round ([Steamworks event tools](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/event_tools)); a roguelike Early Access post-mortem fell behind a promised monthly cadence (anecdotal) ([Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/roguelike-early-access-retention-postmortem)). *Accept:* the cadence and candidate list are in ROADMAP as M6.

All of: funnel targets met in the beta; zero `did_not_reproduce` rejections on valid replays across engines for 30 days; moderation queue reviewed within 72 hours during the beta; bot par, medals, archive and streak live for 30 consecutive days without a voided day; privacy page live; signed installer verified on a clean machine.

**Metrics to watch (v1.0).** Wishlist→sale conversion in week one against the 10–15% median; reviews at day 30; D1/D7 on the web build against the puzzle top-quartile figures; daily submitters as a share of DAU. **Risks.** Refunds if the perceived content is "six machines and a press" — the store page must sell the daily, the archive and the boards, since Steam refunds under two hours and one solo developer saw a 21% refund rate on a short game ([Galaxus](https://www.galaxus.de/en/page/indie-dev-calls-on-steam-to-change-its-refund-policy-43238)); a one-person launch week — pre-write the first three announcements.

## Cross-cutting workstreams

**Competitive integrity** runs from v0.5 to v0.9 and rests on one architectural fact: a submitted score is never trusted, only a replay that the verifier re-simulates. The research adds three residual threats re-simulation does not close — bots (the shipped autoplayer), offline seed search against a known seed, and slowdown abuse — and only the third is irrelevant because the game is turn-based ([TASVideos](https://tasvideos.org/Forum/Topics/22766); [Tildes](https://tildes.net/~games/x1x/the_biggest_cheating_scandal_in_trackmania_history)). The workstream therefore layers policy on cryptography: server-minted seeds with published commitments (0.6.2), one best entry per identity (0.6.1), per-player submission buckets and bounded verification time (0.8.7), tagged Assisted play with equal prominence (0.9.2), hash-collision and timing signals for human review (0.9.4), and a tie-breaker and percentile display that keep a single cheated top score from defining the board (0.5.4, 0.4.1). The rule for rules changes stays as `CLAUDE.md` states it: any change bumps `RULES_VERSION`, and a day whose outcomes a bug has compromised is voided and re-issued, never silently re-scored. Keep raw replays for at least the appeal window; the top-100 retention per seed already does this for the entries that matter.

**Accessibility** builds on an unusually strong 0.2.0 base (keyboard placement, reduced motion that follows the OS, three text sizes, an enforced WCAG AA and CVD audit) and adds, in order: rejection reasons as text (0.3.2, which doubles as the screen-reader string set), full remapping (0.8.5), a handheld text preset meeting the Deck's 12 px recommendation and XAG 101's 18 px PC default ([Steamworks: Steam Deck compatibility](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/steamdeck/compat); [XAG 101](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/101)), gamepad with activation on release (0.8.3), and the DOM mirror (0.8.4). Two rules keep accessibility and fairness compatible: sim speed, pause, single-step, undo-before-run, text, contrast, motion and the screen-reader mirror change nothing about offers or scoring and are therefore allowed everywhere including the ranked daily, while anything that would change offers or add hints inside a run belongs in practice ([GAG Intermediate](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/intermediate/): "Let players choose multiplayer with or without others using competitive-advantage accessibility features"). Add single-step ("advance one tick") to the run controls at any point; it is cheap and answers the largest Opus Magnum debug complaint cluster (no step-back or breakpoints, **0.42**) ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/558990/opus_magnum.md)).

**Performance** is already measured (0 dropped frames at 4× in three environments, script ≤ 0.7 ms/frame on hardware per `docs/STATUS.md`) and the workstream is to keep `tools/perf.ts` in the gate for every milestone that touches rendering (0.3.5, 0.4.4, 0.4.2's offscreen export, 0.8.2's scaling), to re-measure on a real low-end laptop (an open ROADMAP item) and on a Deck or equivalent before claiming Verified, and to budget the verifier: each verification is real CPU, so 0.8.7's worker-with-timeout and the autoplayer-par job (0.4.5) must never block the submission path. Electron browsers "run horribly under Wine/Proton" according to the Boson project's authors, which argues for a native Linux build for the Deck rather than Proton ([Fyra Labs](https://blog.fyralabs.com/introducing-boson/)).

**Community** starts small by design: a Discord with #announcements (read-only), #general, #daily-seed (webhook thread per day, 0.7.5), #replays-and-clips and #bugs-and-feedback, because "one active channel makes a server feel alive, while several quiet ones make it feel dead" ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-set-up-a-discord-server-for-your-game)) and because the verifier's webhook is the only "event system" the budget allows. Recruiting the first hundred players follows the evidence rather than the portals: a free web/itch build with the daily (itch allows HTTPS verifier calls and donations for HTML5 games ([itch.io HTML5 docs](https://itch.io/docs/creators/html5))), the Steam page early to accumulate wishlists, and hand-picked outreach to a few automation and puzzle creators rather than mass key blasts — Balatro's inflection was one streamer and Prickle's best wishlist source was Steam festivals, not portals ([EGW News](https://egw.news/gaming/news/35140/balatro-success-complete-story-from-playstack-to-g-ebUZD_a1E); [Sunbird Studio postmortem](https://sunbirdstudio.itch.io/prickle-jam/devlog/845342/steam-release-postmortem)). Exported clips from verified runs are the marketing asset; the #replays channel crowdsources them.

**Tooling** is the workstream that makes the rest affordable for one person plus a nightly routine. The autoplayer becomes three things at once — balance gate (0.5.5), bot par (0.4.5) and the first Assisted-board citizen (0.9.2) — so every change to it must keep its `origin: "tool"` tag and its determinism across machines (STATUS already records identical results on two machines). Add `tools/funnel.ts` (0.3.7), `tools/review.ts` (0.9.4), the cross-engine golden corpus (0.8.1) and a `tools/void-day.ts` for the announced re-issue path. Keep the Control Room instrumentation alive per `CLAUDE.md`; it is the owner's review surface for the routine's pull requests. Each feature above names its first increment implicitly; when the routine takes one, the STATUS "Next task" should quote the feature id, the acceptance line, and the files it touches, and the routine's rule of "one bounded increment per run, PR per run, never push to main" from `docs/CONTINUE.md` is the right grain for every S item and for each run of an M item.

## Do not do

**No streak loss, decay, login bonuses, countdown-urgency copy or guilt notifications.** These are the temporal and psychological dark patterns of the taxonomy applied to 1,496 mobile games, where only 10.76% of titles reported none and 96.8% of "dark" games were free-to-play ([arXiv 2412.05039](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05039v1)); the FTC's 2022 report names fake countdown timers as deceptive ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers)); Duolingo's own data say forgiveness retains better ([Duolingo blog, streaks](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals)). The daily is "available today", not "lost forever".

**No pay-to-win, premium currency, loot, variable-reward cosmetics, paid streak repair or XP-style volume tallies.** LocalThunk refused gambling mechanics and wrote into his will that Balatro must never be used for gambling ([Wikipedia: Balatro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro)); Luck be a Landlord was built to avoid "predatory microtransactions" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luck_Be_a_Landlord)); XP races corroded Duolingo's leagues into grinding and bots ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/duolingo-app-cheats-hacks-leagues-xp-why-duohacker-1850506482)). Rank is by best verified score only, so attempts cannot buy rank.

**No unlocks, pity timers, pick-steering or rubber-banded targets that change what a daily seed offers or demands.** Each is deterministic in isolation and each breaks the "same board for everyone" promise; Balatro disables unlocks on seeds, Slay the Spire's daily unlocks everything, and the correlated-RNG analysis of Slay the Spire 2 shows how quickly players reverse-engineer offer correlations ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/best-balatro-seeds/); [Steam Community thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/3667553591702749704); [tck.mn](https://tck.mn/blog/correlated-randomness-sts2/)). Cosmetics earned by participation are fine; nothing earned may touch offers, weights, costs or targets.

**No hidden randomness inside the 80 ticks, and no "dry run" preview of the exact result.** The first is output randomness that reads as unfair on a shared seed ([Game Developer: Randomness and Game Design](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/randomness-and-game-design)); the second is the option LocalThunk refused because "people are just going to click on it" and it would turn a casual game into a spreadsheet ([GMTK Substack](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/balatros-cursed-design-problem)). The sim stays legible; the run stays the reveal.

**No second ranked axis shown beside money, and no second currency.** Three simultaneous graphs produced "repetitive replays and self-doubt" for 37% of themed Opus Magnum complaints ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/558990/opus_magnum.md)); the separate reroll currency "just plain sucked" ([Game Maker's Notebook transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/balatro-creator-localthunk-inspirations-jokers/)). Badges are not ladders; rerolls cost score.

**No leagues or divisions until daily submitters reliably exceed roughly 300, and never a weekly demotion zone.** Cohorts of ~30 need population to fill, and demotion-zone anxiety is the most-cited league complaint ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://duolingo.deconstructoroffun.com/mechanics/leagues); [Matt Mecham](https://mattmecham.substack.com/p/what-a-one-year-streak-in-duolingo-taught-me-about-community-gamification)). The 300 figure is an inference.

**No live multiplayer, shared-world co-op, battle royale or public matchmaking.** Live formats need concurrency the game will not have and cost a near-rewrite (Stardew's 15,000+ lines; Spelunky 2's years of netcode fixes), while shapez 2 reached 1.0 at 700K+ sales without official multiplayer ([Chucklefish](https://chucklefish.org/?p=1295); [Steam discussions: Spelunky 2](https://steamcommunity.com/app/418530/discussions/3/3801651941327528270); [COGconnected](https://cogconnected.com/2026/04/shapez-2-1-0-launches-on-pc-following-an-impressive-700k-early-access-sales/)). Tetris 99 needed Nintendo's server team and weekly 99-player tests ([Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/tetris-99-devs-on-how-the-game-came-to-be-scrapped-targeting-mechanics-future-plans-much-more/)). Elimination would also cut six-round runs short and remove the build-and-watch payoff.

**No one-attempt daily (Spelunky style) and no local-midnight reset.** One-shot is the most intense but most criticised and most video-abusable choice, and a mis-click at minute six would waste a day ([The Ludite](https://theludite.com/2014/06/23/spelunky-when-scoring-goes-bad/); [Game Developer: The 24-hour ticket](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-24-hour-ticket-examining-daily-runs-)); local midnight leaks puzzles across zones and breaks travellers' streaks ([Beebom](https://beebom.com/puzzle/when-does-wordle-reset/)).

**No long guided tutorial that changes the real game's progression, and no auto-placing machines for the player beyond the tutorial's fixed starter pieces.** Factorio scrapped an expensive NPE whose "fundament ... was wrong" ([Factorio FFF #327](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-327)); Mini Motorways dropped real-world map data because roads placed for the player weakened ownership ([Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/hustle-and-bustle-how-mini-motorways-built-its-wuselfaktor/)).

**No over-clever placement.** Factorio reverted a smart-belt "roll back on reverse drag" after complaints ([Factorio forums](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?p=679722)); drag paints, dragging back does nothing, erase is explicit.

**No Poki/CrazyGames builds before 1.0, and no switch to Tauri.** Poki blocks all external requests by default and CrazyGames caps the initial download at 50 MB and forbids ads in Basic Launch, so a portal build cannot talk to the verifier without exceptions and would be a separate esbuild target ([Poki requirements](https://developers.poki.com/guide/requirements-quality); [CrazyGames technical requirements](https://docs.crazygames.com/requirements/technical)); Tauri has no maintained Steamworks binding ([GDevelop forum](https://forum.gdevelop.io/t/implementing-steam-achievements-with-tauri/73202)).

**No free-text chat, no real names, no email or birthdate on the web build, and no raw #1 as the headline.** Among Us gated free chat behind accounts with under-13 handling ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/among-us-account-system-safety-harassment-kids-185830761.html)); public boards should avoid real names ([Playable](https://help.playable.com/en/articles/11162624-best-practices-for-using-leaderboards)); Opus Magnum shows the value 1% down the list because the top "may very well be a cheated score" ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/playing-opus-magnum-by-different-rules/)).

## Measurement plan

**Consent and storage.** Telemetry is opt-in (0.3.7), explained in one sentence, linked to the privacy page (0.9.5), and stored on the verifier keyed by a random install id; IP is hashed with a daily salt and raw events are kept 30 days; no layout data and no non-daily seed strings leave the client. The verified-replay pipeline is itself the richest telemetry and needs no client trust: submissions, attempts per identity, shares (a client-side counter sent with consent), days played, group membership and challenge opens are all server-side facts.

**Events (fixed list; add only by editing this list).** `app_start`, `consent_set`, `tutorial_step_start {n}`, `tutorial_step_complete {n}`, `first_draft_pick`, `first_place_attempt`, `first_place_rejected {reason}`, `first_rotate`, `undo_used`, `first_run_start`, `first_run_complete {money}`, `round_complete {n}`, `game_complete {mode}`, `second_game_start`, `daily_start`, `daily_submit_result {verified|rejected:reason}`, `share_copied {variant}`, `clip_exported {format}`, `histogram_around_me_opened`, `challenge_opened`, `fork_started`, `tip_opened {id}`, `settings_changed {key}`, `session_end {minutes}`. The Unity standard-events pattern of start/complete per step is the model ([Unity blog](https://blog.unity.com/technology/standard-events-explained-part-1-onboarding)), and the most-watched edge is `first_place_attempt → first_run_start`, where orientation rules bite.

**Funnel and retention targets.** First session: `first_run_complete / app_start` ≥ 80%, `tutorial_complete / tutorial_start` ≥ 70%, `second_game_start / first_run_complete` ≥ 50%, median rejected placements before the first valid one ≤ 2, median time to `first_run_complete` ≤ 3 minutes — these are synthesised from single-game case studies (13% and 37.9% tutorial completion in two cases ([GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/exploring-gaming-funnels); [Gamerboom](https://gamerboom.com/archives/31845))), not an industry benchmark. Retention on the web build: aim at the top-quartile puzzle figures of about **32% D1 / 11–12% D7 / 4.5% D28** and treat the all-genre median of ~23% D1 / ~4% D7 as the floor ([GameAnalytics Q1 2024](https://investgame.net/news/pdf/mobile-gaming-benchmarks-for-q1-2024/)); on Steam expect a top-quartile D1 of only 15–16% with median sessions around 18 minutes, so a first session should hold several full runs ([GameDevReports: GameAnalytics 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)). Daily health: share of DAU who submit a verified run (≥ 60% of those who finish a daily), median attempts per submitter, share of submitters who copy a share block or export a clip (≥ 15%), 7-day days-played distribution, weekend dip. Launch: wishlist→week-one sales against the 10–15% median; ≥ 50 reviews by day 30; demo→wishlist at the 15–23% norm, without redesigning gameplay because most demo players leave ([How To Market A Game, June 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/06/30/nobody-plays-demos-and-that-is-ok/)).

**Playtest cadence.** One recorded think-aloud round (4–5 fresh testers, 20–30 minutes, one or two questions per round, change only what two or more testers hit) at v0.3, v0.5 (the Sieve and reroll), v0.7 (challenge and groups) and v0.9 (with a screen-reader user and a controller-only user); a Steam Playtest or password itch build from v0.9 for scale, read through the funnel only. Autoplayer and balance sweeps run at every rules bump and are committed under `docs/baselines/<version>/`.

## Evidence quality

**Rests on measured data (strongest).** Forgiving streaks and the cheapest qualifying action (Duolingo A/B deltas: +3.3% D14, +14% D7 for the wager, +4% for the amulet); spoiler-free sharing as a growth engine (Wordle's 90 → ~300k → millions, with share-rate unpublished); retention benchmarks (GameAnalytics 2024–2026); wishlist-to-sales medians and Next Fest correlation (GameDiscoverCo, HTMAG surveys — survey-based and directional); the dark-pattern prevalence study (1,496 games); the near-miss neuroscience (Clark et al., n=40, gambling context, used here only to justify showing the gap to the next threshold, not to engineer near-wins); the cross-engine `Math.sin/cos` divergence (one documented V8-vs-V8 case); Steam's documented thresholds and rules (discounts, Next Fest, Deck, trusted writes); the 37%/42%/21% Opus Magnum review-theme weights (a third-party text analysis, semi-measured).

**Rests on designer opinion (credible, uncontrolled).** The Rube Goldberg payoff and no-dominant-strategy goals (Mega Crit, LocalThunk); the minimal-toolset philosophy (Zachtronics); one rule per step and one guidance channel (Factorio FFFs, Traynor); Wuselfaktor and event-driven audio (Dinosaur Polo Club); satisfying frequent interactions (Dorfromantik); the deleted reroll currency and the 15–25% skip target (LocalThunk); unlimited practice over one-shot (Bénard, Ribbins); friends-first boards (Mega Crit's patch default); the juice canon (Jonasson/Purho, Lovato) with its own authors' warnings.

**Rests on the researchers' and this report's inference (weakest; validate with the game's own telemetry).** Every specific number not quoted from a source: the 48-hour window, the $40 reroll and 10–30% usage band, the 25–50% pick-rate gate, fixed share-glyph thresholds, the 50/80/95 medal percentiles, the 300-submitter threshold for leagues, the ≥ 80/70/50% funnel targets, the ≥ 15% share rate, the six-week update cadence; the Sieve as the right first machine (the archetype evidence is strong, the specific rule is a design proposal); tier gating mapped from Super Auto Pets' two-turn cadence onto six rounds; the claim that GIF/WebM export will matter for a 48-cell factory as it did for Opus Magnum; the screen-reader mirror design (pattern sources are standards, the application to this game is untested); the device-token identity scheme (standard practice, no game-specific case found); and the entire alternating-round co-op idea the multiplayer notes called "novel and unproven", which this plan deliberately leaves out of scope until v1.0 proves the async basics.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what "social" and "addictive" should mean for this particular game. Social is not a lobby; it is a verified replay that a second person can race, watch, fork or beat hours later, and a text block that spreads without spoiling anything — both of which fall out of the determinism Junkyard Draft already has. Addictive, done ethically, is not a streak you fear losing; it is a daily that is always there, a histogram that shows where you stand among real people without inventing any, and a run whose 80 ticks are worth watching because you built them. The healthiest reference designers in the notes — LocalThunk's "play a few times in an hour, then quit", Subset's "am I having fun doing this one?" — describe a bounded session, and a six-round, five-to-eight-minute run already is one.

The sequencing matters more than any single feature. Legibility (v0.3) comes before spectacle (v0.4) because juice on a confusing rule is "tasteless fruit"; spectacle before depth (v0.5) because a histogram and a clip give the first hundred players a reason to return while one machine is being balanced on evidence; the daily's policy (v0.6) before the social layer (v0.7) because challenge links and groups inherit whatever fairness the daily has; and hardening (v0.8) before strangers (v0.9) because the first cheated or desynced board is the one people remember. Each step is small enough for a nightly routine to take as a pull request, and each leaves the identical-seed promise intact — which is the one thing this game must never trade away.
